# Clinical Knowledge: Common Drug Interactions & Co-Administration

## Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid) and Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
- Co-administration: Vitamin C can generally be taken safely with paracetamol. There are no major clinically significant dangerous interactions between standard therapeutic doses of vitamin C and paracetamol.
- Guidance: Follow recommended package dosages and avoid exceeding maximum daily limits (4000mg for paracetamol in adults).

## Vitamin C and Augmentin (Amoxicillin / Clavulanic Acid)
- Co-administration: Vitamin C does not have any known severe adverse interaction with Augmentin (amoxicillin and clavulanate potassium). They can be taken together or during the same course of treatment.
- Guidance: Complete the full prescribed course of Augmentin as directed by the doctor or pharmacist. Take Augmentin at the start of a meal to reduce potential gastrointestinal upset.

## Paracetamol and Ibuprofen
- Co-administration: Paracetamol (analgesic/antipyretic) and Ibuprofen (NSAID) work via different mechanisms and can be used together or alternated for short-term pain or fever management when single-agent therapy is insufficient.
- Precautions: Avoid taking multiple paracetamol-containing products simultaneously (e.g., cold/flu remedies) to prevent accidental paracetamol overdose.

## Antibiotics and Alcohol
- Moderate to severe interactions exist with certain antibiotics (e.g., Metronidazole, Tinidazole), where alcohol causes severe nausea, vomiting, flushing, and tachycardia.
- For standard penicillin-class antibiotics like Amoxicillin or Augmentin, alcohol does not directly block the antibiotic mechanism, but is strongly discouraged as it impairs immune recovery, causes dehydration, and increases stomach irritation.

## Antacids and Oral Medications
- Antacids containing aluminum, magnesium, or calcium can bind to certain antibiotics (such as fluoroquinolones and tetracyclines), reducing absorption. Space antacids at least 2 hours apart from oral medications.
